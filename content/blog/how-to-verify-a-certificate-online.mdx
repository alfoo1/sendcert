To verify a certificate online, open the verification link or code printed on it, and check that the page is on the issuer's official website and shows the same name, course and date. If the certificate has no link, contact the issuing organization directly. As an issuer, you can make your certificates verifiable by giving each one a unique public link; SendCert does this automatically when you send from a free account.

## What does it mean to verify a certificate?

**Verifying a certificate** means confirming, with the issuer, that it was really issued to this person for this course on this date. A PDF on its own proves very little, because anyone can edit a PDF. Verification moves the proof from the document to a source the reader trusts: the issuer.

There are three common ways:

- **A verification link or QR code** on the certificate, pointing to a page on the issuer's site
- **A certificate ID** you can look up on the issuer's website
- **Contacting the issuer** by email or phone and asking them to confirm

## How do I check if a certificate is real?

If someone sends you a certificate, for example a job applicant, a contractor or a new staff member:

1. **Find the link, QR code or ID** on the certificate or in the email it came with.
2. **Check the domain.** The verification page should be on the issuer's website or on the official domain of the platform they used, not on a look-alike address.
3. **Compare the details.** Name, course title, issue date and issuer should match the PDF exactly.
4. **Check the status.** Look for an expiry date or a note that the certificate is no longer valid.
5. **If anything doesn't match**, or there's no link at all, contact the issuer using contact details from their official website, not from the certificate itself.

## What does a verification link prove, and what doesn't it?

| A verification page proves | It does not prove |
|---|---|
| The issuer recorded this certificate | That the course was accredited |
| The recipient's name, course and date | The quality of the training |
| When it was issued (and expiry, if any) | That the person is who they claim to be |
| That the PDF wasn't invented from scratch | Skills beyond what the course covered |

A verification link answers "is this certificate genuine?" It doesn't answer "is this course any good?" or "is this the same person?". For identity, ask for ID, as you would for any document.

## How do I make my certificates verifiable?

If you issue certificates yourself, the simplest approach is a unique, public page for each one. In SendCert:

## Should a verification page show the recipient's email?

No. A verification page should show only what a verifier needs: name, course, issuer, dates and status. Email addresses, phone numbers and scores don't belong on a public page. SendCert's verification pages don't show the recipient's email.

## Do certificates expire?

Some do. Safety, first-aid and compliance certificates often have a validity period, because the training needs refreshing. Course-completion certificates usually don't expire in any meaningful sense, but stating a date still helps reviewers judge how recent the training is. A good verification page shows "valid until" for certificates with an expiry date, and marks expired ones clearly.

## When does a verification link matter most?

- **Hiring and contracting**, when an employer needs proof of a required training
- **Continuing education**, when a licensing board audits hours (see [continuing education certificates](/blog/continuing-education-certificates))
- **Compliance trainings** in schools, nonprofits and public agencies
- **Sharing online**, because a link is easier to check than an attachment

For small, informal courses it's a nice extra. Our guide to [online course certificate best practices](/blog/online-course-certificate-best-practices) covers when it's worth it.

## Frequently asked questions

### How can I verify a certificate without a link?

Contact the issuing organization through the contact details on its official website and ask them to confirm the name, course and date.

### Can a verification page be faked?

A fake page can look convincing, which is why you should check the domain. A real verification page lives on the issuer's site or on the official domain of the platform they use.

### Do recipients need an account to view their verification page?

No. Verification pages are public. Anyone with the link can open them.

### Can I add verification links to certificates I already sent?

Only to certificates sent from a free account, which are saved with a link. Certificates sent as a guest weren't stored, so they don't have one.
