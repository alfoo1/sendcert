Picking a certificate template is one of those choices that looks minor and is not. A cluttered design makes your course feel amateur. A plain design makes students feel like you cranked it out in ten minutes. The template you pick sets the tone of the entire hand-off.

Here are five template styles that work for most online courses, what kind of course each one fits, and how to actually customize them.

## 1. Elegant formal

A traditional layout with a serif title, a double frame and fine accent rules on a white background. A clear line for the recipient name and two signature lines at the bottom.

- **Best for:** professional development, leadership programs, awards, formal course completions.
- **Why it works:** serif type and a symmetric frame signal credibility. It reads like a diploma, which is what many graduates want to post on LinkedIn.
- **Customize:** pick an accent color that fits your organization (navy for law and finance, burgundy for academics), change the signer, keep the body copy formal.

## 2. Modern minimalist

A mostly white design with a thin frame, one accent color and a clean sans-serif title. Generous whitespace, a simple line for the name, and two signature columns.

- **Best for:** tech courses, bootcamps, staff trainings, design and marketing classes.
- **Why it works:** it looks like it came from a modern product, not a Victorian schoolhouse, and it prints well in black and white.
- **Customize:** the accent color does most of the work. Choose your brand color and the whole template follows it.

## 3. Friendly workshop

A rounded frame, a playful label above the title and a few light decorative touches.

- **Best for:** workshops, summer programs, kids and school events, volunteer recognition, any course where warmth matters more than formality.
- **Why it works:** it reads like a thank-you, not a diploma, which matches what these events actually deliver.
- **Customize:** the accent color changes the mood a lot. Orange feels energetic, teal calm, violet creative.

## 4. Your own design

The most flexible option. Design a single PNG or JPG in Canva, Figma, or Photoshop — whatever you already use — and upload it as the template. Just leave visible blank space where the name will go, and a tool like [SendCert](/app) will overlay each student's name on top automatically.

- **Best for:** courses with strong existing branding, cohort programs that already have their own visual identity.
- **Why it works:** consistency with the rest of your course materials. If your landing page is orange-and-charcoal, your certificate should be too.
- **Customize:** everything. Use your real logo, your brand typeface, your specific course imagery.

## 5. Utilitarian / quick

A plain template for when you just need to get certificates out the door. White background, one clean typeface, no ornaments, recipient name and course title only.

- **Best for:** internal training, compliance-style completions, short workshops where a fancy certificate would feel excessive.
- **Why it works:** no-nonsense matches what internal learners expect. Nobody is framing this one; they are saving the PDF to prove they took the training.
- **Customize:** barely any. That is the point.

## How to customize any of these

Regardless of which template you pick, you will want to edit at least:

- **The institution name.** "Example Academy" is not your course name.
- **The certificate title.** "Certificate of Completion" is the default, but "Certificate of Achievement," "Workshop Diploma," or your own wording can set a different tone.
- **The body text.** The one-liner before the recipient's name ("This is to certify that…") is your chance to mention the specific course.
- **The signer.** Usually your name or the institution's name, under the signature line.
- **The accent color.** Borders, headings, and decorative lines all pick up the accent color. One slider changes the whole mood.

## Adding a logo

If you have a small logo PNG (transparent background), drop it into the top corner of the certificate. A good logo makes a big difference — students recognize it, and it ties the certificate back to your course brand. Keep it small (under 60 px tall) so it does not compete with the title.

## Adding extra fields

Beyond the recipient name, you may want to show:

- The course name (if the template body copy is generic).
- The completion date.
- A grade, score, or honors designation.
- An instructor signature.

These go on top of the template as additional text fields. You set the position, size, and color for each one, just like you do for the name.
