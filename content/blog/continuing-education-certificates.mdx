A continuing education (CE) certificate documents that a person completed a learning activity and how much credit it is worth. It should show the participant's name, the activity title, the date, the number of contact hours or CEUs, the provider, and an approval number if the activity is accredited. To issue them at scale, filter your attendance list to people who met the requirements, then generate and email one personalized PDF per person.

Below is what each part means, how to calculate credit, and how to send certificates to a whole group without retyping names.

## What is a CEU?

A **CEU (Continuing Education Unit)** is a standard measure of continuing education. Under the widely used IACET standard, **1 CEU equals 10 contact hours** of instruction. A 3-hour workshop is therefore 0.3 CEUs.

Many licensing boards don't use CEUs at all. They count **contact hours**, **clock hours** or **credit hours** directly, and some have their own definitions (for example, how many minutes make one hour). Always state credit in the unit your participants' boards expect, and if you are unsure, list the contact hours. People can convert hours; they can't convert a vague certificate.

## What must a continuing education certificate include?

| Field | Example | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Participant name | Dana Rivera | Must match the name on their license |
| Activity title | Trauma-Informed Practice in Schools | Boards check that the topic is relevant |
| Date(s) | March 12, 2026 | Credit must fall inside the renewal period |
| Credit | 3.0 contact hours (0.3 CEU) | The number the participant reports |
| Provider | Riverbend Training Institute | Who is vouching for the hours |
| Approval number | Provider #12345 (if accredited) | Lets a board confirm the approval |
| Format | Live online | Some boards limit self-paced credit |
| Signature | Program director | Shows who is responsible |

Only include an approval or provider number if you actually hold one for that activity. If you are not an approved provider, say nothing about approval. Participants may still be able to self-report the hours, depending on their board.

## Do I need to be an accredited provider?

It depends on who your participants report to. Some boards accept certificates from any reputable provider; others only accept activities from approved providers. Accreditation comes from the relevant board or accrediting body, not from the tool you use to make certificates.

## How do I calculate the hours?

1. **Count instructional time only.** Start with the scheduled time, then subtract breaks, meals and registration.
2. **Use real attendance.** For online sessions, check each person's time in the session. For in-person sessions, use sign-in and sign-out times.
3. **Apply the board's rounding rules.** Some round to the nearest quarter or half hour; others don't allow rounding up.
4. **Set a minimum.** Decide in advance what share of the session someone must attend to receive credit, and apply it consistently.
5. **Convert if needed.** Divide contact hours by 10 for CEUs.

## Attendance or completion: which certificate should CE participants get?

If credit depends on finishing several sessions or passing a post-test, issue a certificate of completion. If credit depends only on being present, a certificate of attendance with the hours stated is fine. Our guide to [attendance vs. completion vs. achievement](/blog/certificate-of-attendance-vs-completion) explains the wording for each.

## How long should I keep CE records?

Requirements vary by board and by accrediting body, and some ask providers to keep records for several years. Keep, at minimum:

- The attendance list and how you calculated hours
- The course outline and the date it ran
- A copy of the certificate design and the list of who received one

## Common mistakes with CE certificates

- **Rounding up generously.** It's the fastest way to have credit rejected.
- **Listing an approval number for the wrong activity.** Approvals are often activity-specific.
- **Using registration lists instead of attendance.** People who registered but didn't attend shouldn't get credit.
- **Leaving out the date.** Boards need to know the credit falls inside the renewal period.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many contact hours is 1 CEU?

Under the IACET standard, 1 CEU equals 10 contact hours. Some boards use their own units, so state contact hours as well when you can.

### Can I issue CE certificates for a webinar?

Yes, as long as you can confirm who attended and for how long. Use your webinar platform's attendance report, not the registration list.

### Does SendCert approve courses for CE credit?

No. SendCert makes and emails the certificates. Approval comes from the board or accrediting body your participants report to.

### Can I put different hours on different certificates?

Each batch uses the same extra fields for everyone. If people earned different hours, split them into separate lists and send one batch per group.
