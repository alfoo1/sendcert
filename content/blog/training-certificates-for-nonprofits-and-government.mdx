Nonprofits and public agencies can issue training certificates without buying a learning platform: keep an attendance list, create one certificate design, and send each person a personalized PDF by email. A free tool like SendCert handles the certificates and delivery, while your sign-in sheet and the certificate records give you what you need when someone asks for proof.

This guide covers what to include, how to run the process after each training, and how to keep records that hold up.

## Why do nonprofits and public agencies issue training certificates?

Because many of their trainings are required, and someone eventually asks for proof. Common examples:

- **Child-safety and mandated reporter trainings** for staff and volunteers who work with minors
- **Workplace safety** trainings (first aid, emergency procedures, equipment)
- **Onboarding** for new employees and volunteers
- **Policy trainings**: data privacy, ethics, harassment prevention
- **Professional development** for teachers and program staff

A certificate gives each person their own proof, and gives the organization a consistent record for audits, grant reports, insurance reviews and partner requirements.

## What should a staff training certificate include?

- **Full name** of the staff member or volunteer
- **Training title**, specific and recognizable ("Recognizing and Reporting Child Abuse", not "Annual Training")
- **Date completed**, and the **duration** or hours
- **Organization or department** that delivered it
- **Trainer or coordinator** name or signature line
- **Expiry or renewal date**, if the training must be repeated

| Training type | Certificate type | Useful extra fields |
|---|---|---|
| Mandated reporter / child safety | Completion | Hours, renewal date |
| Volunteer onboarding | Completion | Program name, date |
| Safety briefing | Attendance | Date, duration, location |
| Policy training with quiz | Achievement | Date, "passed assessment" |
| Conference or seminar | Attendance | Event name, hours |

Not sure which type to use? See [certificate of attendance vs. completion vs. achievement](/blog/certificate-of-attendance-vs-completion).

## How do I issue certificates after a training session?

1. **Take attendance digitally.** A shared spreadsheet or a simple online form gives you names and emails without retyping a paper list.
2. **Confirm who qualifies.** Remove anyone who left early or didn't finish the required parts.
3. **Open SendCert** and choose a built-in design, or upload your organization's certificate with its logo and seal.
4. **Place the name** and add extra text fields for the training title, date, hours and renewal date.
5. **Add people**: upload the list as CSV or Excel, or paste names and emails directly.
6. **Write a short email** with `{{name}}`. For example: "Hi {{name}}, thank you for completing today's training. Your certificate is attached. Please keep it for your records."
7. **Send a test to yourself**, check every detail, then send to the group.

Guests can send up to 30 certificates a month. For larger teams, a free account (just your email, no password) raises the limit to 300 a month and keeps a history of every send.

## How do we keep records for audits?

Auditors and funders usually want to see who was trained, on what, and when. Keep:

1. **The attendance list** for each session, with the date
2. **The training materials** or agenda, showing what was covered
3. **A record of the certificates issued**

If you send from a free SendCert account, your dashboard shows every send, and each certificate gets a public verification link that anyone can open to confirm it's genuine. That's useful when a partner organization or a school asks a volunteer to prove a training. See [how to verify a certificate online](/blog/how-to-verify-a-certificate-online).

Public agencies may also have records-retention rules that apply to training records. Check with your records officer how long to keep them and where.

## What about privacy?

Training lists contain personal information, so collect only what you need: a name and an email are enough for a certificate.

- **Guest sends** in SendCert aren't stored. The list is used to create and deliver the certificates, and that's all.
- **Sends from a free account** store each certificate (recipient name and email, training title, dates) so it can be verified. Verification pages show the name, training, issuer and dates, not the email address.

Choose the mode that matches your organization's policy.

## Can volunteers and contractors get certificates too?

Yes, and they often need them more than staff. Volunteers who serve several organizations are frequently asked for proof of child-safety or background-related trainings. A PDF they can keep, plus a verification link, saves everyone a round of emails.

## How can a small team keep this manageable?

- **Create one design per training type** and reuse it, changing only the title and date.
- **Send certificates the same day** as the training, while the attendance list is fresh.
- **Use renewal dates** for trainings that expire, and put a reminder in your calendar.
- **Batch by session.** One send per session keeps records clean and easy to find.
