Certifier is a legitimately good product. If you are running an accredited program with thousands of credential holders, verifiable badges, and an audit trail — Certifier, Accredible, and similar platforms earn their price tag.

But most educators landing on this page are not running that kind of operation. They are running a course or a workshop, they have a few dozen students, and they want to hand out a nice-looking PDF at the end. $45 a month, $540 a year, for that is a tough sell.

Here is what Certifier does that you probably don't need, and a simpler free option for when the fancy features are overkill.

## What Certifier actually gives you

Certifier's paid tiers include:

- **Unique verification URLs** per certificate.
- **Public credential pages** the student can link to.
- **LinkedIn "Add to Profile" one-click integration.**
- **Bulk issuing via CSV** (also standard in free tools).
- **Analytics** — who viewed, who shared.
- **Revocation** — invalidate a certificate after the fact.
- **API access** for programmatic issuance.
- **White-label** branding options.

All of this is genuinely useful — for the right use case. That use case is organizations issuing formal credentials where verifiability is a product requirement.

## What course creators usually need

Walk through what you actually do with the certificate:

1. Student finishes the course.
2. You send them a PDF.
3. They save it, maybe print it, maybe share it on LinkedIn as an image.

That's the full arc for 90% of independent course creators. Verification pages, revocation, credential analytics — none of that is touched.

So the real question is: does your course need formal credentialing, or does it need a clean PDF delivered by email?

## When Certifier makes sense

You should probably pay for Certifier if:

- You run an accredited training program with external audits.
- Employers actively check your students' credentials.
- You issue thousands per month and need bulk operations + API access.
- Credential revocation matters (e.g., compliance certifications that expire).
- Your brand needs the "enterprise credentialing platform" optics.

In those cases $45/month is a small line item.

## When it doesn't

You probably do not need Certifier if:

- You run a self-made course with a few dozen to a few hundred students.
- Your certificate is more of a thank-you than a credential.
- You don't need revocation, analytics, or LinkedIn integration.
- You want to spend five minutes setting up, not an afternoon configuring a platform.

## The simpler alternative

[SendCert](/app) is built for the case Certifier is overkill for. The feature set is narrow on purpose:

- Upload a template (your design, or pick a built-in).
- Upload a CSV of recipients.
- Write an email body with a `{{name}}` placeholder.
- Send.

That's the whole product. No credit card. You can send up to 30 certificates a month as a guest, without an account. A free account (just your email, no password) raises that to 150 per month, which covers most cohort-based courses, and gives every certificate a public verification link.

What it skips:

- Badge graphics for LinkedIn (PDF only).
- Analytics dashboard.
- Credential revocation.
- API access (not yet).

If those absences are dealbreakers, the enterprise platforms earn their price. If you read that list and nothing jumped out as important, you probably do not need to pay $540/year.

## The decision tree

```
Do you need formal credentials (revocation, audit trails, badges)?
├── Yes → Certifier or Accredible
└── No → Do you send more than 500 certificates/month?
         ├── Yes → Certifier (for API + scale)
         └── No  → SendCert or similar free tool
```

Most solo course creators land in the bottom branch.
