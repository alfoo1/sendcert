A professional development (PD) certificate is a teacher's proof that they completed a PD activity and how many hours it was worth. It should state the teacher's name, the session title, the date, the PD hours, the provider (school, district or organization) and a signature. Districts can issue them in minutes by turning the session's sign-in sheet into a list and emailing each teacher a personalized PDF.

Here's what to include, how to count hours fairly, and how to get certificates to every teacher the same day.

## Why do teachers need PD certificates?

In most US states, teachers renew their license by completing a set amount of professional development within a renewal period. States use different names (PD hours, clock hours, credits, professional growth points) and different rules about which activities count.

Teachers are usually responsible for keeping their own proof. A clear certificate saves them from chasing the PD office months later, and saves the PD office from reissuing certificates one by one. Requirements vary by state and district, so check your state's licensing agency for the exact rules your teachers follow.

## What should a PD certificate include?

- **Teacher's full name**, matching their license
- **Session or course title**, specific enough to show relevance ("Supporting Multilingual Learners in Grades 3–5", not "Tuesday PD")
- **Date(s)** of the activity
- **PD hours** (or your state's unit), stated as a number
- **Provider**: the school, district or organization running the session
- **Delivery format**: in person, live online or self-paced, if your state distinguishes them
- **Signature** of the PD coordinator, principal or presenter
- **Activity or course number**, if your district or state assigns one

## How do you count PD hours?

1. **Start with scheduled time** for the learning activity itself.
2. **Subtract breaks, lunch and logistics.** Twenty minutes of setup isn't PD.
3. **Use sign-in and sign-out times** for long sessions, so a teacher who left at noon doesn't get a full day.
4. **Round consistently**, following your state's or district's rule.
5. **Write the number on the certificate.** "6 PD hours" is far more useful to a teacher than "full-day session".

## How can a district send PD certificates to every attendee?

The bottleneck is usually the list, not the certificate. A practical workflow:

1. **Collect attendance digitally.** A Google Form or a shared sheet at the door gives you names and emails in a spreadsheet instead of a paper list.
2. **Check eligibility.** Remove anyone who didn't attend long enough.
3. **Open SendCert** and pick a built-in design, or upload your district's certificate with its logo and seal.

5. **Add teachers**: upload the spreadsheet as CSV or Excel, or paste names and emails straight in.
6. **Write a short email**: "Hi {{name}}, thank you for attending today. Your PD certificate for 6 hours is attached."
7. **Send a test** to yourself, check it, then send to everyone.

## How should PD certificates be worded?

Match the wording to what you verified. Our guide to [attendance vs. completion vs. achievement](/blog/certificate-of-attendance-vs-completion) goes deeper, but these cover most PD:

| Session type | Certificate type | Example line |
|---|---|---|
| One-day workshop | Attendance | "attended *Classroom Management Strategies* for 6 PD hours" |
| Multi-week course | Completion | "has completed *Literacy Coaching Cohort*, 20 PD hours" |
| Course with assessment | Achievement | "successfully completed and passed the *Data Literacy* assessment, 10 PD hours" |
| Conference | Attendance | "attended sessions at *Spring Teaching Conference*, 8 PD hours" |

## What are common mistakes with PD certificates?

- **Generic titles.** A licensing reviewer can't tell whether "PD Day" was relevant.
- **Missing hours.** The single most important number is often left off.
- **Late delivery.** Certificates sent weeks later get lost. Send them the same day.
- **Wrong names.** Use the names teachers typed themselves, not a nickname list.
- **One certificate for the whole staff.** Each teacher needs their own document with their name.

## Should PD certificates go to a personal or school email?

School email is fine for daily use, but teachers change districts. Consider telling teachers to save their certificates in a personal folder, or let them choose which email to use on the sign-in form. A PDF attachment means they can keep it anywhere, without needing an account to open it.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many PD hours do teachers need?

It depends on the state and the license type. Check your state's education or licensing agency for the current requirement and renewal period.

### Can a school issue its own PD certificates?

Usually yes, for school-provided PD. Some states require activities from approved providers for certain credits, so check before you promise credit.

### What if a teacher attended only part of the session?

Give them a certificate with the hours they actually attended, or none if they didn't meet your minimum. Split the list and send separate batches if hours differ.

### Can I use our district's own certificate design?

Yes. Upload it as a JPG or PNG, leave space for the name, and add the extra fields you need on top.
