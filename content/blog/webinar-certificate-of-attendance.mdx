To send webinar certificates of attendance, export the attendance report from your webinar platform, keep the people who attended long enough, and generate one personalized PDF per person. With a tool like SendCert you upload or paste that list, place the name on a design, and every attendee gets their certificate by email in a few minutes.

The details matter, though: who counts as an attendee, what the certificate should say, and when to send it.

## What is a webinar certificate of attendance?

A **webinar certificate of attendance** confirms that a person joined a live online session. It states their name, the webinar title, the date and usually the duration. It doesn't claim they learned anything or passed a test; that would be a certificate of completion or achievement. Our guide to [attendance vs. completion vs. achievement](/blog/certificate-of-attendance-vs-completion) explains the difference.

Attendance certificates are popular because they give attendees something to show their employer, help them log training hours, and give organizers a reason for people to stay until the end.

## How do I get the list of who actually attended?

Use your platform's **attendance or participant report**, not your registration list. Zoom, Microsoft Teams, Google Meet and most webinar tools can export who joined, often with join and leave times. Whether the report is available, and how detailed it is, depends on your plan or edition, so check your platform's help center before the event.

A typical report gives you:

| Data in the report | What to use it for |
|---|---|
| Name | The name on the certificate (clean it up first) |
| Email | Where to send the certificate |
| Join and leave times | Calculating time in session |
| Total duration | Applying a minimum-attendance rule |

If your platform doesn't export emails, match attendees to your registration list by name, or ask people to confirm their email in the chat or a short form at the end.

## Should everyone who registered get a certificate?

No. Registration means intent, not attendance. Set a rule before the webinar and apply it to everyone, for example:

- Attended at least 75% of the live session, or
- Attended at least 45 minutes of a 60-minute webinar

State the rule in your reminder email, so attendees know what they need to do. People who only watched the recording are a separate case: some organizers send them a different certificate ("viewed the recording"), others send nothing.

## How do I create and send the certificates?

1. **Export the attendance report** as CSV or Excel.
2. **Clean the list.** Remove duplicates (people who rejoined appear twice), hosts and panelists, and anyone below your minimum. Fix obvious name typos and capitalization.
3. **Open SendCert** and choose a built-in design or upload your own.
4. **Drag the name into place** and add extra text fields for the webinar title, date and duration ("60-minute live webinar").
5. **Add your logo** so the certificate matches your event branding.
6. **Upload the cleaned file** or paste names and emails directly.
7. **Write the email** using `{{name}}`, and include the recording link and slides if you share them.
8. **Send a test to yourself**, then send to all attendees.

If you have more than 30 attendees, sign in with a free account first (just your email) to raise the limit to 150 a month. For very large events, see [how to send 100 certificates at once](/blog/how-to-send-100-certificates-at-once).

## What should the email say?

Keep it short and useful:

> Hi {{name}},
>
> Thanks for joining *Building Inclusive Classrooms* today. Your certificate of attendance is attached.
>
> The recording and slides are here: [link]. Hope to see you at the next one.

A good certificate email doubles as your follow-up email. Attendees open it, because it contains something they want.

## When should I send webinar certificates?

The same day, ideally within a few hours. Attendees are most likely to download, save and share the certificate while the session is fresh, and same-day delivery avoids "where's my certificate?" emails.

To make that realistic, prepare the design and email before the webinar. Afterwards you only need to export the report, clean it and send. That takes minutes.

## How do I handle certificates for a webinar series?

For a series, you have two options:

- **One certificate per session.** Simple, and attendees get something each time.
- **One certificate at the end.** Track attendance across sessions in a spreadsheet, then send a certificate of completion to people who attended all (or most) sessions.

For the spreadsheet side, our guide to [creating certificates from Excel or Google Sheets](/blog/certificates-from-excel-or-google-sheets) shows how to prepare the list.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I give certificates to people who watched the recording?

You can, but word it differently ("viewed the recording of…") and don't count it as live attendance if hours matter for credit.

### Do webinar certificates count for continuing education?

Only if the board your attendees report to accepts the activity. If you offer CE credit, see our guide to [continuing education certificates](/blog/continuing-education-certificates).

### What if an attendee's name is misspelled in the report?

Fix it in the spreadsheet before sending. People often join with nicknames or device names, so a quick check is worth it.

Yes, if you send from a free SendCert account. Each certificate gets its own public verification link.
