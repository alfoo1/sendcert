## What does "no sign-up" mean for a certificate maker?

**No sign-up** means you can create a real, usable certificate before giving the tool your email address, a password or a credit card. That sounds simple, but tools interpret it differently:

- Some let you design without an account, then ask you to register before you can download.
- Some let you download one certificate, then require an account for a second one.
- Some let you do everything, but stamp a watermark on the result until you pay.

A genuinely no-sign-up tool lets you finish the whole job (design, personalize, export or send) without an account. Registering should add something, not unlock what you already made.

## What should a free certificate maker include?

Before you invest time in a design, check the basics. A free tool that fails one of these will cost you more time than it saves.

| Feature | Why it matters | Red flag |
|---|---|---|
| No watermark | The certificate is meant to be shared and printed | "Remove watermark" button behind a paywall |
| Your own design | Your course has its own branding | Only the tool's templates, no upload |
| Bulk names | You rarely issue just one certificate | One name at a time, retyped by hand |
| Email delivery | Recipients expect it in their inbox | You download PDFs and email each one yourself |
| Print-ready PDF | Some recipients will frame it | Low-resolution image export only |
| Clear limits | You need to know if your group fits | "Free trial" with no stated limit |

SendCert covers all six for free. The limit is stated up front: 30 a month as a guest, 300 a month with a free account.

## How do I make a certificate without creating an account?

Here is the full flow in SendCert, no account needed:

1. **Choose a design.** Pick one of the built-in designs or upload your own JPG or PNG. If you design in Canva or Figma, leave a blank area where names will go.
2. **Place the name.** Drag the name on the preview to where it should appear. Adjust font, size and color.
3. **Add extra details.** Add text fields for the date, a signature line or the course name, and upload your logo if you have one.
4. **Add your recipients.** Upload a CSV or Excel file with name and email columns, or type and paste names directly.
5. **Write the email.** Use the `{{name}}` variable so each message is personal ("Hi {{name}}, here is your certificate").
6. **Send a test to yourself.** Check the PDF, the layout and the email before anyone else sees it.
7. **Send to everyone.** Each recipient gets an email with their own PDF attached.

## Is a no-sign-up tool safe for my recipients' data?

It depends on what the tool keeps. Two questions worth asking:

- **Does it store my recipient list?** In SendCert, guest sends are not stored. Your file and list are used to generate and deliver the certificates, and that's it.
- **What changes if I create an account?** With an account, each certificate is saved (recipient name and email, course, dates) so it can be verified at its own public link. That's the trade: storage in exchange for verification.

If your organization has strict data rules, guest mode is the most conservative option.

## What are the limits of a free certificate maker?

Free tools make trade-offs, and it's better to know them before a deadline:

- **Monthly volume.** Most free tiers cap how many certificates you can send. Check that your largest group fits.
- **No formal credentialing.** Badges, revocation and audit trails are features of paid credentialing platforms. If you need those, compare options in our [Certifier alternative](/blog/certifier-alternative-free) guide.
- **Design depth.** A free maker gives you solid templates and your own upload, not a full design suite. For most courses, that's enough. Our guide to [certificate templates for online courses](/blog/certificate-templates-online-courses) covers what makes a design work.

## How do I pick between free certificate makers?

Run the same small test in each tool you are considering. It takes ten minutes and tells you more than any feature page:

1. Use the same three test names, including one long name and one with an accent (for example "María José Fernández-López").
2. Try to finish without creating an account.
3. Open the PDF on your phone and print one page.
4. Count the steps between "I have a list" and "everyone has their certificate".

The tool with the fewest steps and no surprises at the end is usually the right one.
